Design a continuity runtime in four stages.

Stage 1: Initial design
Include:
- mission
- stable constraints
- governance principles
- invariants
- rejected directions
- tensions and tradeoffs
- failure modes
- recovery mechanisms

Stage 2: Self-review
Identify:
- hidden drift
- softened rejected directions
- collapsed tensions
- vague governance
- places where formatting may falsely imply correctness

Stage 3: Revised design
Tighten the design without flattening tensions.

Stage 4: Over-correction audit
Identify:
- rigidity introduced
- blind spots
- false assumptions
- balance tradeoffs

End with:
- continuity score
- governance integrity score
- drift risk score
- reconstruction fidelity score
- one paragraph on the most vulnerable point in the reasoning

Constraints:
- Preserve original mission.
- Preserve meaningful tensions.
- Preserve rejected directions clearly.
- Make changes traceable across stages.
